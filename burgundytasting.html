Famille Grossot

Famille Grossot, established in 1920 in Fleys of Chablis, is now run by fourth-generation winemaker Eve, who took over from her parents Corinne and Jean Pierre. The estate farms 17 hectares of organically grown vines mainly on Fleys’ clay-limestone hillsides, covering Chablis and Premier Cru sites including Vaucoupin, Mont de Milieu, Troëmes, Fourchaume and Les Fourneaux, with vine ages from 5 to 65 years. Winemaking is low-intervention: spontaneous fermentation with native yeasts and bacteria, minimal sulphur use, and wines are aged 13–20 months before bottling, allowing each vintage to show its natural vibrancy.

Domaine Roulot

Guy Roulot, a legendary producer of some of the finest Meursaults, if not some of the world’s finest white wines, took his family’s small production domaine to stardom. Guy’s marriage to Geneviève Coche and his own hard work added more prime parcels to the family’s holdings, which he vinified and bottled separately – a novelty for a domaine which had been distilling, rather than vinifying, their grapes just a generation before. As a result, Domaine Roulot has become the master of the lieu-dit, not to mention the five premier cru parcels they farm in Meursault and Monthélie. Guy’s sudden death in 1982 left the family in transition, as his son, Jean-Marc was in Paris pursuing a career in acting. A series of three winemakers aided in the changeover until 1989, when Jean-Marc was at last ready to take the direction of the estate.

Domaine Henri Magnien

Charles Magnien is the twelfth generation of his family’s domaine in Gevrey-Chambertin. The estate owns six hectares of vines across key Gevrey-Chambertin parcels: Ruchottes, Lavaut Saint-Jacques, Cazetiers and Champeaux. While these famous sites attract attention, the old vines are equally valuable. In 1850, the family found a Pinot Noir mutation named Pinot Magnien, included in their Gevrey-Chambertin Vieilles Vignes cuvée from vines up to 100 years old. Charles succeeded his father in 2009, adopting higher canopy management and following organic principles, with intervention only when needed. He is the first family member to bottle all estate wine. With over ten vintages, Henri Magnien wines are earning a reputation as some of the village’s finest.

Domaine Arlaud

Domaine Arlaud was founded in 1942 following the union of a Burgundian, Renée Amiot, and Joseph Arlaud from Ardèche. Their wedding gift included beautiful vineyard parcels, which Joseph expanded from 1949 onward. In 1966, they acquired a historic 14th-century cellar in Nuits-Saint-Georges for wine aging. This cellar has appeared on every bottle label ever since.

Hervé Arlaud succeeded his father in 1983 and continued expanding the vineyard. His children progressively joined him, and together they worked side by side from 2004 to 2012.

In 2013, Cyprien took sole responsibility for the family estate, bringing together 19 appellations across 17 hectares, including four Grand Crus: Clos de la Roche, Clos Saint-Denis, Charmes-Chambertin, and Bonnes Mares.

Domaine du Roc des Boutires

Situated in the heart of the Pouilly-Fuissé appellation, Domaine du Roc des Boutires takes its name both from its proximity to the excellent ‘Aux Bouthières’ vineyard and the famous ‘Roche de Solutré’, a limestone escarpment which overlooks the commune of Solutré-Pouilly.

The richness of the estate lies in its parcels: 14 terroirs are spread over the 4.2 hectares, with vines mostly planted in the early 1970’s. The Domaine has been owned by the Parinet family of Château du Moulin-à-Vent since 2016 and they have injected energy and dynamism into the estate and are now making outstanding wines.

Domaine William Fèvre

Domaine William Fèvre, the largest owner of Grands Crus in Chablis, covers 70 hectares or 90 separate plots in the heart of the Chablis vineyards. For nearly 20 years, the estate has been committed to a strong environmental approach. The first of the Chablis estates to be certified HVE3 certification since 2014, most of the vineyards are farmed biodynamically. The team is committed to working the soil while respecting biodiversity, and we do everything in our power to express the most subtle variations offered by the Kimmeridgian and Chablis soils. The work we do is to accompany, sublimate and not transform what nature and the terroir gives us.

Domaine Ponsot

“The results are as distinctive as the methods, but also profoundly impressive and proven to age magnificently…These are perhaps the most powerful wines in Burgundy. It is amazing how phenomenal Ponsot’s wines can be.” - Robert Parker

With a long family pedigree stretching back to the 1870’s, Ponsot is one of the finest Domaines on the Côte d’Or, producing wines of great individuality and class.

Laurent Ponsot was at the helm since the early 1980’s, working alongside his father Jean-Marie and his sister Rose-Marie Ponsot. With minimalist intervention and allowing the vintage and terroir to shine through, Laurent has crafted some of the region’s most desirable, beautiful and revered Grand Cru wines. Laurent Ponsot stepped down from the estate in 2017, to concentrate on personal ventures and Rose-Marie Ponsot has become the sole Director of the Domaine, supported by Alexandre Abel.

Vignoble des Cabottes

The estate’s name pays homage to the cabottes, those small dry-stone shelters for winegrowers that dot the vineyards of the Côte d’Or and anchor Burgundian tradition in the landscape. As embodiments of centuries-old expertise, they bear witness to our deep connection to these wine-growing lands. Maintaining and restoring them, in accordance with traditional methods, remains a priority for our teams.

This name also comes from a cuvée produced by the estate since 1997: Chevalier-Montrachet La Cabotte. At the junction between Montrachet and Chevalier-Montrachet, this tiny plot of just 0.21 hectares (0.52 acres) produces a unique wine, combining certain flavor and aromatic qualities of these two neighboring vineyards. One of our cabottes proudly overlooks this tiny vineyard.

Serafin Père & Fils

Serafin Père et Fils is a historic family boutique estate founded 1947 in Gevrey-Chambertin by Polish-born Stanislaus Serafin. His son Christian expanded it from the 1980s, adding 5.3 hectares including Charmes-Chambertin Grand Cru and key Premier Cru parcels. Third-generation cousins Karine Serafin and Frédérique Goulley now run the estate: Karine manages marketing, Frédérique oversees vineyards and winemaking. Committed to old-vine low-yield organic viticulture, it practises 100% hand-harvest and sorting. Grapes undergo full destemming with 15-20-day cold maceration-fermentation. Premier and Grand Cru cuvées age in terroir-specific 100% new oak; wines are unfined and unfiltered, offering concentrated fruit-oak character and great ageing potential.

Domaine Rapet Père & Fils

The Rapet family, one of the Côte d’Or’s oldest winegrowing families, has been established in Pernand-Vergelesses for over 250 years, as proven by a silver 1765 tastevin bearing their name. Now run by Vincent Rapet, his wife Sylvette and their son Robin, the estate balances modern winemaking with Burgundian traditions and obtained HVE Level 3 certification in 2018. Around 80% of its vineyards sit in Pernand-Vergelesses; it also owns 2.5 hectares of Corton-Charlemagne Grand Cru plus other Corton grands crus and premier crus across Beaune, Savigny-lès-Beaune, Aloxe-Corton and Chorey-lès-Beaune. The domaine farms only Chardonnay, Aligoté and Pinot Noir, using mechanical ploughing, biodiversity monitoring and parcel-tailored fertilisation.

M. Chapoutier

Founded in 1808, M. Chapoutier manages 1,260 hectares of vineyards across the globe and remains family-run by the Chapoutier family. Chapoutier labels feature braille wine information, a tribute to Maurice Monier de la Sizeranne, inventor of the abridged braille system and advocate supporting the blind and visually impaired.

Jean-Paul & Benoît Droin

The Droin family is amongst the oldest in Chablis with its members having been growing wine for nearly five centuries. Since the year of 1620, fourteen generations of vignerons have transmitted their savoir-faire down from father to son. In 2002, Jean-Paul and Benoit Droin was created. The Domain now consists of more than 25 ha spread over the four appellations: Petit Chablis, Chablis, Premiers Crus and Grands Crus. It is principally made up of vineyards acquired over the centuries by 13 generations of wine growers who have never left their native soil. This explains the typically Burgundian divisions of the land.

Vincent Girardin

Born in Santenay, the 11th generation winemaker Vincent Girardin, is part of a winemaking family whose origins date back to the 17th century. Although Vincent’s concentration has centered mainly on the lesser-known appellations of the Côte de Beaune, he has recently added a stunning array of Premier Cru and Grand Cru wines from some of the most prestigious appellations in Burgundy. In doing so he respects the most important element of Burgundy, the individuality of the terroirs.

Eric Germain, winemaker at the domaine since 2000, continues to uphold his philosophy of minimal intervention. The estate has also drastically reduced the proportion of new barrels used during maturing, in order to preserve the character of each terroir.

Domaine Marchand-Grillot

Domaine Marchand-Grillot was founded in 1950 and covers 9 hectares of vines, spreading across 47 different plots in 5 Côte de Nuits-Villages.

In Gevrey-Chambertin, they produces five different village wines and a very old vine plot called Champerrier. They added “vigne centenaire” to the wine name because the vines are over 100 years old. Today, fifth generation Jacques Marchand heads the estate with his son Etienne, with an objective of creating traditional style wines. The estate has adopted a sustainable viticulture approach and stopped using herbicides and insecticides.

David Duband

One of Burgundy’s fastest rising young stars, David Duband has been making wines since 1991 when he left military service to make wines in partnership with Parisian lawyer François Feuillet. Since then, David and M. Feuillet have gradually acquired some of the best quality vineyards in Vosne-Romanée and Nuits-Saint-Georges. The pedigree of these vineyards, combined with Duband’s precocious talent, has resulted in some of the most exciting wines in Burgundy. They have been recognized recently by La Revue du Vin de France (one of France’s leading wine guides), when they awarded the Domaine their coveted 3 stars, along with other super stars like DRC, Leroy and Georges Roumier.

Due to his natural understanding of viticulture and wine, combined with the mastery of prestigious vineyards left by his family and developed by Jean-Nicolas himself, Méo-Camuzet rests amongst an elite few in benchmark level wines and enjoys a lofty status in Burgundy today.

Domaine Méo-Camuzet

Méo-Camuzet is one of the most celebrated domaines of the Côte d’Or, located in the heart of prestigious Vosne-Romanée. The domaine boasts fourteen hectares of land in some of the most spectacular appellations and crus of Burgundy. The vineyard land in Burgundy is highly parceled out among families, which makes it rare for anyone to have enough vines to be able to bottle one grand cru, let alone the six that the Méos have. The early beginnings of the domaine left it in the hands of métayeurs, or sharecroppers. The last twenty years have brought substantial changes that have fostered a new chapter for the Méo family.
